Advanced conceptual questions

A1. §5.2 calls J-Prime an optional Tier 2 that the architecture is "deliberately resilient" to losing. §93.6 makes it the only lane, with paid lanes switched off by declaration. What second-order effects follow when the optional Mind becomes the single point of failure, and should the system still "degrade gracefully"?

In April, resilience came from the cascade: if J-Prime was unhealthy, DoubleWord was promoted, and Claude sat behind it (§5.2, §12.1). With JARVIS_PAID_LANES_ENABLED=false, Claude and DoubleWord are not even constructed (§93.6). There is nothing to fall back to.

Three effects follow. First, failure classification matters more. §93.5 shows a 402 read as an outage, which produced 125 false awaken triggers in one session. With one lane, a misclassified failure leaves nowhere to send the work. Second, the Mind now shares a single GPU with the Soul: training takes the card through an exclusive lease, so the lane is also unavailable whenever learning runs (§93.6, §93.11). Third, Principle 2's habit of "mark degraded and continue" (§3) stops making sense, because there is no lesser lane to continue on.

A defensible position is that, with one lane, the right behaviour is to refuse and queue, not to degrade. October's design leans this way. Capability assurance refuses to run an operation below its declared envelope rather than degrading silently (§93.12). The cost governor already queues work rather than executing it when the budget is gone (§8.2, clause 5). The rehearsal tier records what was exhausted and when it lifts, but it is default off (§93.5). The evidence that would settle it is whether ops queued during an outage or lease later land at the same rate as ops that never waited.

A2. §5.3 defines Reactor Core as the sandboxed execution runtime and says that separation "is what makes the Iron Gate's containment guarantees meaningful". §93.11 describes JARVIS-Reactor as the training repository. If the Soul's job changed, where did containment go, and what happened to the "uncommon" Mind-to-Soul channel of §5.4?

In April, the Soul was where code ran: run_tests and permitted bash executed inside Reactor, with resource limits, process isolation and syscall filtering (§5.3). By October, §93.11 says the Soul, "JARVIS-Reactor, the training repository", had been "architecture on paper", and it now trains a GRPO LoRA adapter on what O+V recorded. The §93.16 corrections table does not list this change, so the reader has to reconcile the two descriptions.

Containment did not disappear. It moved to mechanisms the October text names on the Body side. Operations execute in a sovereignty worktree (§93.7). Test runs execute in a PID namespace (§93.8). A resource-aware execution lock stops a test suite from out-of-memory-killing a soak (§93.8). Modules that execute on import are quarantined (§93.8). So the §5.3 claim survives in purpose but not in location.

The Mind-to-Soul channel that §5.4 called "uncommon", allowed "for capability-graduation workflows", became central. The Training Lifecycle Handoff leases J-Prime, verifies the VRAM release, trains, converts and publishes the adapter to J-Prime's registry, then activates it or rolls back (§93.11). The second-order effect is contention: Mind and Soul now compete for one GPU, and the lease is the protocol that keeps them from both holding it.

A3. The Saga guarantees "fully applied or fully reverted" at APPLY (§7.2). §93.7 adds later stages: accumulation branches, a promotion gate that can quarantine a landing, and fast-forward-only promotion to local main. Does Saga atomicity survive these later stages?

The Saga's guarantee is scoped to APPLY and the CrossRepoVerifier before commit (§7.2, §7.3). Once each repo's change is committed, the paper's atomicity argument has done its job. §93.7 then describes promotion as a per-branch pipeline: accumulation onto ouroboros/battle-test/<ts>, a promotion gate checking provenance, coverage and vanished public symbols, quarantine on failure, and fast-forward to local main.

The paper describes that path for JARVIS and does not say whether promotion is coordinated across the three repos. If the gate quarantined the Body half of a contract change while the Mind half was promoted, local main in the two repos would disagree. That is the very partial state §7.1 exists to prevent, recreated one stage later.

A cautious position: until promotion is shown to be repo-aware, a cross-repo landing should be treated as atomic only up to commit. The evidence that would settle it is either code showing the promotion gate groups commits by operation ID across repos, or a test that quarantines one half of a 2c.1 landing and checks what happens to the other.

Challenge problems

Challenge 1 (adversarial). A 2c.1 Saga renames a request field from user_id to uid in jarvis/backend/protocols/prime.py and in jarvis-prime/api/v1/schemas.py, the two files from §7.1. Both patches validate on their own, both apply, and the CrossRepoVerifier passes. But a module in jarvis-reactor also reads user_id, and it is not in the Saga. Find every layer in Parts II and III that might catch the third consumer, and decide honestly whether any of them does. Then propose, and label as a proposal, the smallest change that would.

Hint

Look at what CONTEXT_EXPANSION does with a path outside the repo (§10.1, step 4), and at which tests VERIFY selects (§16.1).

Worked solution

Go through the candidate layers.

  • CONTEXT_EXPANSION (§10.1). Suggested paths are resolved against the repo root, and paths outside the repo are rejected by the Iron Gate. Even if the model guessed the Reactor consumer, expansion could not add it to context. The model is structurally unable to look across the boundary through this phase.
  • Saga application (§7.2). Compensation only covers repos that appear in the patch dictionary. Reactor is not one of them, so nothing there is applied or reverted.
  • CrossRepoVerifier (§7.2). It "executes cross-repo tests to validate that the union of the patches is consistent". It catches the break only if its test selection includes Reactor tests that touch the field. The paper does not say how that selection is made, and the scenario says it passed.
  • VERIFY (§16.1). It runs a scoped set of tests that exercise the changed files. The Reactor consumer is unchanged, so its tests are not in scope.
  • Stale-exploration guard (§15.3). It compares hashes of the target files only. It is irrelevant here.

Honest conclusion: as described, no layer is guaranteed to catch it. The design would ship a contract break into the third repo and leave it for a later sensor, such as a test-failure signal, to find.

Proposal (not in the paper): when a candidate changes a symbol named in a cross-repo contract, the CrossRepoVerifier's scope should be every repo in the Service's _repo_registry (§6), which already lists jarvis, jarvis-prime and jarvis-reactor, not just the repos in the patch. A cheap first step is a deterministic search for the old field name across all registered repos before GATE, failing closed on any hit outside the Saga. This is a structural check, not a model call, so it fits Principle 5 (§3). To check the proposal, plant exactly this scenario and confirm the operation ends in POSTMORTEM rather than COMPLETE.

Challenge 2 (systems design). Under the October constraints (one 32 GB GPU, no paid lanes), the Service state machine of §6.1 has no honest way to say "the only lane is lent to training". Givens from §93.6 and §93.11: J-Prime cold-loads in 15 s to 18.9 GiB resident; the lease releases 21,236 MiB; Reactor holds the 30B at 15.64 GiB resident while training. (a) Show with the numbers why serving and training cannot share the card. (b) Explain why neither DEGRADED nor FAILED describes a lent lane. (c) Design the missing behaviour and justify it against Principle 2 and §8.2.

Hint

Re-read the definitions of DEGRADED and FAILED in §6.1 word for word. Then look for an existing "queue rather than execute" rule in Part III.

Worked solution

(a) Serving needs 18.9 GiB and training needs 15.64 GiB, a total of 34.54 GiB. A 32 GB card holds at most 32 GiB even on the most generous reading, and less (about 29.8 GiB) if GB means 10^9 bytes. Either way, the two jobs do not fit together, which is why the lease is exclusive. The lease frees 21,236 MiB (about 20.7 GiB), which is more than the 15.64 GiB that training needs. Returning to service costs at least the 15 s cold load.

(b) §6.1 defines DEGRADED as "some providers unhealthy but operations still flowing". With one lane lent, no generation can flow, so DEGRADED would mislead the operator. FAILED is "a terminal failure state that prevents further operations". A lease is planned and temporary, so marking it terminal would be wrong, and it would block the return.

(c) Proposal (not in the paper): add a non-terminal state, for example LEASED, entered from ACTIVE when the training lease is granted and left back to ACTIVE when J-Prime's reload is confirmed. While LEASED:

  • Intake keeps accepting signals, but the pool does not dispatch GENERATE. This mirrors §8.2 clause 5, where an exhausted budget queues the next operation instead of running it.
  • In-flight operations either finish before the lease is granted or are recorded as aborted, never leaked (Principle 1, §8.1).
  • The transition is written to the ledger and shown in the cockpit, which already has a GPU-lease lock screen (§6.1, §93.9).

Justification: Principle 2 forbids blocking boot chains and hanging on a slow dependency. LEASED does not hang; it states plainly that one resource is away and resumes when it returns. It also matches capability assurance, which refuses to run below the declared envelope rather than degrading silently (§93.12). To check the design, run a training handoff during a soak and confirm three things: no operation reaches GENERATE while LEASED, no operation is lost, and queued operations dispatch within roughly the cold-load time after release.
