Quick check · §92.1

1. Of the four Phase B subagents, only GENERAL is wrapped in the Semantic Firewall and the mutation cage. What in its description explains why it gets the heaviest guard?

EXPLORE is read-only and REVIEW is a shadow-mode critique, so neither changes anything. GENERAL is the infrastructure subagent driven by its own LLM (JARVIS_GENERAL_LLM_DRIVER_ENABLED, now default-true) and able to use tools that mutate. That is why it gets injection and credential detectors, a recursion ban, an output quarantine fence, a hard-kill wrapper and a per-instance mutation budget.

2. A hard kill ends a GENERAL run abruptly. What keeps the record of what it did from dying with it?

The state_mirror dict. It keeps exec_trace outside the killed run, so the audit trail survives even when the cage terminates the subagent (§92.1).

Quick check · §92.2

1. Four sensors (RuntimeHealth, WebIntelligence, ProactiveExploration, Scheduled) stayed on polling. Is that unfinished migration?

No. The paper says those four stay on polling "by design", calling it "architectural, not deferred". The other 9 of 13 moved to events: webhooks for GitHub and CI, fs.changed.* on the event bus for filesystem sensors, and the conversation bus for IntentDiscovery.

2. The observability route module may not import the orchestrator, policy or Iron Gate code, and this is grep-enforced. What property does that protect?

It keeps the surface read-only in a structural sense. A module that cannot import the decision-making code cannot influence decisions, whatever a request asks for. Loopback-only binding and a 120/min/IP rate limit narrow who can ask at all.

3. The SSE stream is bounded (8 subscribers × 64 queue × 512 history) and drops the oldest events. Why bound it, and why send a single stream_lag event?

Without bounds, a slow or stuck client could make the organism buffer without limit. Dropping the oldest event protects the server. The stream_lag event tells the client it missed events, so it can resync with Last-Event-ID replay from the 512-event history instead of silently showing a wrong picture.

Quick check · §92.3

1. PostureObserver runs every 300 s but applies 900 s of hysteresis. What does the hysteresis buy?

It stops the posture from flapping between values on every noisy reading. A new posture has to persist well beyond one observation before it takes over. The 0.75 confidence bypass lets a strong, unambiguous signal switch sooner, and operator overrides are clamped to 24 h so they cannot linger forever.

2. The SensorGovernor's emergency brake fires on cost_burn>0.9 OR postmortem_rate>0.6. Why OR rather than AND?

Each condition is dangerous on its own. Spend running away is a reason to slow down even if ops succeed, and a high postmortem rate is a reason to slow down even if it is cheap. Requiring both would let a cheap failure loop, or an expensive successful one, run at full rate. Either trigger drops caps to 20%.

3. Why does a registry of 481+ JARVIS_* flags need typo detection?

An environment variable with a misspelled name is simply ignored, so the operator believes a feature is on or off when it is not. Bounded Levenshtein matching turns JARVIS_POSTUR_ENABLED into a suggestion of JARVIS_POSTURE_ENABLED, so the silent failure becomes visible.

Quick check · §92.4

1. On SIGHUP, SIGINT or SIGTERM the harness writes a partial summary.json before shutdown. Which failure class does this close?

Sessions killed from outside used to leave only debug.log and no auditable artifact. Now they leave a summary with session_outcome=incomplete_kill and a signal-specific stop_reason. The schema change is additive, so existing readers keep working.

2. What does --headless skip, and when does it switch on without being asked?

It skips the SerpentREPL input task. It switches on automatically when sys.stdin.isatty() is false, as in agent-run soaks, CI and daemons. It replaces the old trick of piping tail -f /dev/null into the process to keep stdin open.

Quick check · §92.5

1. Why did the status line never appear before the TTY-gate fix?

should_render() asked sys.stdout.isatty(). Under patch_stdout(raw=True), sys.stdout is a patched stand-in that does not report a TTY, so the check always failed. The fix asks sys.__stdout__, the unpatched original stream.

2. Tool bodies, diffs and op blocks get monotonic refs (t-N, d-N, o-N) that are never reused, even after drop-oldest eviction. What would reuse break?

A ref the operator saw earlier could silently point to a different artifact after eviction, so /expand t-12 might show the wrong thing. With no reuse, an old ref either resolves to its original artifact or to nothing. The prefix also lets one /expand verb route to the right store.

3. The "I'm going to do X" intent prose comes from a fire-and-forget LLM call with a 50-token cap, a 5 s timeout and hard-fail-silent behaviour. Why all three limits?

Narration exists to make the organism legible, and it must never slow or break the operation it describes. The call is small, short and allowed to fail without a trace. For the per-tool "why" line the channel uses a deterministic descriptor table with no LLM cost at all.

Quick check · §92.6

1. Five new REPL verbs shipped with 0 edits to repl_dispatch_registry.py. How?

The registry's naming cage registers verbs automatically by convention. A new verb only has to follow the naming rule. Nobody edits the central dispatcher, which removes a shared hot spot for merge conflicts and mistakes.

2. Temporal Observability took about 2 h against a 3-day audit estimate. What explains the saving?

Composition. It is a thin single-slice wrapper over the existing CausalityDAG, which only gained three read-API helpers (nodes_for_phase, first_record_in_phase, distinct_phases). Almost nothing new had to be built.

Quick check · §92.7

1. All thirteen slices shipped default-FALSE, with 977/977 tests green. Why ship tested code switched off?

Phase 9 cadence discipline: the substrate lands first, and each flag graduates later on the cron schedule by clean soaks (§92.9). Passing tests is not the graduation criterion.

2. phase_orchestra.py derives each phase's note by modular arithmetic on phase_index, with "no hardcoded per-phase mapping". What does that protect against?

Drift. A hand-written per-phase table would go stale the moment the canonical 11-phase tuple changed. Deriving the note from the canonical tuple keeps the surface correct automatically. It is the same move as confidence_aura doing "zero parallel logprob math".

Quick check · §92.8

1. The artifact contract is wired at a single AST-pinned choke point before pctx.merge_artifacts. What does it catch there?

Four violation classes as phase artifacts are merged: UNKNOWN_KEY (a rename), TYPE_MISMATCH (shape drift), WRONG_PRODUCER (a phase writing a slot it does not own) and SCHEMA_VERSION_SKEW. One choke point means no artifact can enter the shared context by a side door.

2. Vector #11 gates the CuriosityScheduler on time.monotonic() but keeps wall-clock time as ts_epoch. Why both?

Wall-clock time can jump when NTP corrects it, which could make the scheduler fire twice or stall. A monotonic clock never jumps, so it is safe for gating. Wall-clock time is still kept for the audit record, where a human needs real dates.

3. Vector #10's TOCTOU coverage uses multiprocessing.Process. Why not threads in one process?

The race being guarded is between processes taking the AutoCommitter's file-lock critical section. Only real separate processes exercise a cross-process lock honestly. The paper calls it "true cross-process race coverage".

Quick check · §92.9

1. Two runner soaks halted the cron install before it happened. Why halt, instead of installing the cron and fixing the runner in parallel?

The cron produces graduation evidence. A cadence on a broken runner would generate verdicts from a broken instrument. So the sequence was halt, diagnose, ship Slices 4 and 5, re-prove green, and only then install the cron.

Quick check · §92.10

1. Layer 2 made --once delegate to the wrapper script. Why does that matter?

Before, --once carried its own copy of the environment block, so a fix like Layer 1's .env loader could land in one path and not the other. Now cron, launchd, --once and manual runs share one source of truth.

2. In Layer 3 the YAML had been purged, yet it still blocked DoubleWord. How?

JARVIS_TOPOLOGY_SENTINEL_ENABLED defaulted to false, and in that state the v1 YAML's dw_allowed:false applied as a blanket block. The gate flag was never set on the cadence path, so the cadence ran an older configuration than everyone assumed.

Quick check · §92.11

1. In Visual VERIFY, a red TestRunner clamps a visual pass to fail, but not the other way round. Why asymmetric?

A screenshot that looks right is weaker evidence than a failing test. Letting a visual pass override a red test would let a broken change through. Letting visual evidence only add failures can make the check stricter, never looser.

2. What is the auto-demotion rule for, given that Visual VERIFY has graduated?

If the graduated check produces 50% or more false positives after graduation, it is demoted automatically. A noisy gate that blocks good work is a cost too, and the rule stops it from lingering at full authority.

Quick check · §92.12

1. What do the three open questions have in common?

Each takes a property the substrate shows empirically and asks for it as a proof. The three are Iron Gate determinism, the Anti-Venom recursion bound and Reverse Russian Doll containment. Each is paired with a formal framework: certifiably robust networks, Wang's Markov-chain RSI, and SICA plus ASL-4.

2. Why can't Wang's RSI formulation be applied to O+V as it stands?

Wang's chain is stationary. O+V is non-stationary (its rules change with history), spans several repositories, and is memory-augmented. The open question is whether the bounded-RSI claim survives an extension to that setting.

Quick check · §92.13

1. O+V already had a parallel generator (§M9). What does treefinement add that parallel repair lacked?

Cross-branch learning. The validator prunes losing branches, and the survivors inform the next layer's GENERATE prompt. Linear repair passed only one predecessor's failure forward, so a wrong turn at iteration N spoiled iterations N+1 to 5. Independent parallel repair passed nothing between branches.

2. Why was v3.3's strategy gate "structurally REACHABLE but BEHAVIORALLY a no-op"?

No production factory had been registered, so selecting tree mode had nothing to run. v3.4 added the factory, the git apply adapter and lazy boot registration, which made the gate operational end to end.

3. Why may repair_engine.py import repair_tree_production only lazily, inside _invoke_tree_factory?

It avoids a circular import, and callers that never use tree mode pay nothing for it. Together with the sha256 pin on _run_inner, it keeps the legacy linear path byte-identical.

Quick check · §92.14

1. A three-file candidate passes files 1 and 2 and fails on file 3. What state does the repository end in?

The state it started in. Files 1 and 2 are restored from pre-apply snapshots, and any newly created files are unlinked. This batch-level rollback is the answer to SWE-Bench Pro's multi-file atomicity failure mode.

2. Why does it matter that score_evaluation needs only (captured_patch, problem) and not the original worktree?

Scoring becomes reproducible from disk. A sweep can be re-scored when the rubric evolves, two rubrics can be compared without re-running the agent, and third parties can score patch-problem pairs themselves.

3. Phase E wraps each task in its own try/except. What happens to a task that throws?

It becomes a synthetic record, INGEST_FAILED or SCORING_ERROR, with the exception class in diagnostic, and the sweep carries on with the remaining tasks. One bad instance cannot lose the run, and the failure is still on the record.

Quick check · §92.15

1. Which of the five template elements stops a new capability from quietly building its own copy of an existing primitive?

Composition over fork. Every new primitive must compose at least two existing canonical surfaces, and parallel implementations are rejected by AST pins. For example, SWE-Bench-Pro used the canonical get_semaphore and flock_append_line instead of its own.

2. PRD §40.7.6 says the citations give "baseline numbers to benchmark against". Name the two baselines.

Constitutional Classifiers' 86% to 4.4% jailbreak reduction is the target for Anti-Venom self-immunization. SWE-Bench Pro's 43.6% for Claude Sonnet 4.5 is the resolve-rate benchmark.

Quick check · §92.16

1. How do O+V's safety classifiers differ from Anthropic's Constitutional Classifiers?

They are AST-deterministic, not learned. They use no LLM, take about 10 ms per detection and fail closed on a parse failure. The analogy is structural (classifiers deployed around a generator), not a claim of the same method.

2. Which of the five threads have landed citation-to-implementation closures, and which are only positioned?

Formal verification (Treefinement, porting AlphaVerus) and benchmarks (the SWE-Bench-Pro harness) have closures. RSI, constitutional safety and recursion-bounding are positioned structurally, with their full empirical regimes gated on Phase 9 soaks.

3. Live-SWE-agent is named as the closest published parallel. What difference does the paper call operationally significant?

The Anti-Venom hardening overlay. Live-SWE-agent does not appear to expose an equivalent.

Quick check · §92.17

1. Describe one Reverse Russian Doll iteration in terms of C, δ, Sn and M.

The core C generates a candidate patch δ under the shell-injected prompt. The shell Sn filters and refines it to δ′, or rejects it. The mutation operator M then extends the shell with the validated patch, for example a new AST pin, rule or test, giving Sn+1.

2. Property (a) says the shell's deterministic entropy never decreases. Which assumption about M carries that claim?

That every Sn+1 strictly extends Sn, so M only adds structure and never removes it. If any iteration could delete or relax a constraint, monotonicity would fail.

3. Why does the ExplorationLedger make M non-stationary in Wang's sense?

Each Sn+1 depends on the whole history S0 to Sn through the ledger and the recency-weighted SemanticIndex. So the transition rule changes over time, which breaks Wang's fixed-kernel assumption. The dissertation conjectures a "non-stationary-but-monotone" extension.

Quick check · §92.18

1. Why does the paper say formal methods become load-bearing at the Neurosurgeon threshold, not before?

Before it, the system does not modify its own governance, and adversarial soaking such as the 0/38 cage can show it holds. At Neurosurgeon the system modifies its own governance. Then only a proof can rule out some modification trajectory that escapes the bound, because no finite set of observations can.

2. Match the three §92.12 open questions to the three Penn lineages.

Iron Gate determinism goes to Wong (certifiably robust networks). Anti-Venom recursion-bound stability goes to Roth (trustworthy-learning foundations). Reverse Russian Doll containment as a whole goes to PRECISE (end-to-end verifiable AI systems).
