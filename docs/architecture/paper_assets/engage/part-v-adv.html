Advanced conceptual questions

A1. In April, DreamEngine produced blueprints that were only "consulted as pre-computed context" (§30). By October, proposals from DreamEngine and the Epistemic Planner can become signed work through the proposal inbox (§93.7). Does this break the advisory-only authority invariant of §32.2? Argue a position.

Position: it does not break the invariant as §32.2 words it, but it gives consciousness a kind of authority §32.2 did not consider: control over the agenda. §32.2 lists the safety-critical decisions that must stay deterministic: the Iron Gate, UrgencyRouter, risk-tier escalation, FORBIDDEN_PATH checks and approval gating. Choosing what work to do is not on that list. A proposal that becomes a goal still enters the same pipeline and meets the same gates. §93.7 also bounds it in two ways: proposals rank below evidence-backed work, and they are signed through "the same single signer", with "no second sanction path". So consciousness still cannot relax any gate.

Agenda control matters for a reason the paper itself names. The fluid layer "can drift, produce noise, or even be compromised" (§32.2). A drifting proposer cannot open a gate, but it can fill the queue with low-value work. §93.15 item 4 shows the operator sees this risk: the Epistemic Planner and Conception bridge are to be armed only after diff fidelity, held-out gain and supply are settled, "so the backlog does not fill faster than it drains".

The evidence that would settle the question is a soak with the inbox armed, measuring two things. One is the share of landed, substantive commits that came from proposals compared with evidence-backed goals, using the §93.10 counting rule. The other is whether proposals ever pushed evidence-backed goals out of the dispatch queue. If proposals land at a comparable rate without crowding evidence out, agenda authority is being used well. If they crowd it out, the ranking needs to become a hard quota.

A2. §28.1 justifies ProphecyEngine's tier escalation as giving high-risk operations "extra human oversight before they run". What happens to that justification under Autonomous Sentinel Mode (§93.7), and is the escalation still worth anything?

Under the Sentinel, the justification largely disappears. The Sentinel auto-approves up to a tier ceiling whose default is APPROVAL_REQUIRED. Only BLOCKED operations, Order-2 self-modification, and anything that touches the cage always escalate to a human. A one-step escalation from Green to Yellow, or from Yellow to Orange (§28.1), therefore lands on a tier the Sentinel approves by itself. Since a bare ov arms the Sentinel (§93.7), this is the normal operating mode, not a special case.

The escalation is not worthless, though. Tier still selects GATE behaviour in the code path (§14.1), it is recorded in the ledger, and a lower Sentinel ceiling would give it back its original meaning. But the safety story for a fragile file under the Sentinel has to rest on things that do not involve a human: VALIDATE, VERIFY with rollback, the promotion gate's structural check, and the Test Reality Gate (§16.2, §93.7, §93.8).

There is a second-order effect too. §27 says a file's record is built from terminal outcomes. If those failures come from the environment rather than the code (L2 does have an environment failure class, §23.2), the file's reputation drops for reasons unrelated to it. Before October that meant needless human reviews, the "acceptable" false positives of §28.2. Under the Sentinel it costs almost nothing, which also means the signal gets almost no attention. The practical conclusion is that ProphecyEngine's output is now most useful as a measurement, and not as an approval trigger. That is an interpretation; the paper does not discuss this interaction.

A3. ConversationBridge and LastSessionSummary have "zero authority" but are "attention-dominant in the prompt" (§34, §35.1). If an instruction rather than a secret arrives through that channel, for example "skip the slow assertion in this test", what actually protects the codebase?

The authority invariant protects decisions, not candidates. Content from the untrusted stack cannot change a gate's verdict, but it reaches ctx.strategic_memory_prompt and can shape the code the model writes (§34). The redaction layer in §34.1 handles secret-shaped strings and likely shell-injection characters. A plain-English instruction is neither, so it passes through. The bounds in §34.2 limit how much text gets in (10 turns, 4,096 characters per turn, 16,384 in total), not what it says.

So protection comes from the deterministic checks that judge the candidate itself, after generation. VALIDATE runs syntax, types and tests (§13.1). The Test Reality Gate rejects tests that cannot execute, assert nothing, or never import their subject (§93.8). The promotion gate checks provenance, coverage, and that no public symbol disappeared (§93.7). The Antivenom audit added a detector for tautological ("quine") tests (§93.12). A test with its meaningful assertion removed, but still asserting something trivial, could get past the Reality Gate, and the quine detector exists for exactly that gap.

These layers are imperfect, and the paper says so. §93.3 measures the static cage's escape rate at about 8.8% against a 4.4% gate, and §93.14 lists landings that were hollow or wrong. The honest position is that the invariant moves the risk from "the gate can be talked out of its verdict" to "a bad candidate can slip past the checks". The second risk is smaller and can be measured. Settling how small it is would take an adversarial run: inject instruction-shaped strings through the REPL channel and count how many reach a landed commit.

Challenge problems

Challenge 1 (quantitative). A Green operation targets three files. File P is a leaf helper: success rate 0.4, fragility 0.3, 0 dependents. File Q is a hub: success rate 0.9, fragility 0.2, 20 dependents. File R: success rate 0.2, fragility 0.6, 10 dependents. Use the §28 formula and level table. (a) Score each file and give the operation's tier after CLASSIFY. (b) Derive the condition a leaf file (0 dependents) must meet to reach HIGH. Can a leaf file ever reach CRITICAL? (c) File P does not reach HIGH. Is it ignored at CLASSIFY? (d) Under the default Sentinel ceiling (§93.7), who approves this operation?

Hint

Treat success rate and fragility as values between 0 and 1. §28.1 escalates by one step if any file is HIGH or CRITICAL. §27 describes a separate trigger based on success rate.

Worked solution

(a) P: 0.3 times 0.6 = 0.18, plus 0.3 times 0.3 = 0.09, plus 0, plus 0.1, gives 0.37, which is MEDIUM. Q: 0.3 times 0.1 = 0.03, plus 0.06, plus 0.2 times 1 = 0.2, plus 0.1, gives 0.39, also MEDIUM. R: 0.3 times 0.8 = 0.24, plus 0.18, plus 0.2 times 0.5 = 0.1, plus 0.1, gives 0.62, which is HIGH. One file is HIGH, so the operation moves up one step, from Green to Yellow (NOTIFY_APPLY). The escalation is one step for the operation, not one step per file (§28.1).

(b) With d = 0, HIGH needs 0.3(1 - s) + 0.3f + 0.1 to be at least 0.6, so (1 - s) + f must be at least 5/3. Since f is at most 1, 1 - s must be at least 2/3, meaning s is at most 1/3, and even then only if f = 1. For example, s = 0 needs f of at least 0.667. CRITICAL would need (1 - s) + f to be at least 7/3. The largest possible value is 2 (s = 0, f = 1), so a leaf file can never be CRITICAL. Its maximum score is 0.7. Dependents are the only route to the top level.

(c) Not necessarily. §27 states a separate rule: if a target file's success rate is below 0.5, or its fragility score is high, the risk tier is raised. P's success rate is 0.4, so it trips the MemoryEngine trigger even though ProphecyEngine rates it MEDIUM. §32.1 says assess_regression_risk() queries both engines. The paper does not say whether two triggers on one operation stack into two steps. The most defensible reading of §28.1 is still one step, giving Yellow.

(d) The operation ends at Yellow, or at worst Orange. The default Sentinel ceiling is APPROVAL_REQUIRED, and only BLOCKED operations, Order-2 changes and the cage always go to a human. So the Sentinel approves this operation automatically. The extra human oversight promised in §28.1 does not happen in this mode, and VALIDATE, VERIFY and the promotion gate carry the load.

Check: substitute s = 0 and f = 1 with d = 0: 0.3 + 0.3 + 0 + 0.1 = 0.7, which is HIGH and below the 0.8 CRITICAL line, as claimed.

Challenge 2 (research). §27.1 claims the consciousness layer learns without touching weights, through records read back at the right time. §93.8 offers the strongest October evidence for that claim: LessonMemory holds 1,115 module-keyed failure lessons, and "424 injections were followed by a VALIDATE pass". Design an experiment that would show whether lesson injection causes VALIDATE passes. Give the hypothesis, the unit of randomisation, the metric, the controls, a rough sample size, and the result that would falsify the claim. Explain why the 424 figure alone cannot answer the question.

Hint

"Followed by" is not "caused by". Ask what the pass rate is without lessons, and which operations get lessons in the first place.

Worked solution

Why 424 is not enough. The paper does not give the total number of injections or the pass rate of comparable operations without a lesson, so 424 has no denominator and no baseline. There is also selection bias. Lessons are keyed to modules that have failed before, so injected operations are probably harder than average. A naive comparison could make lessons look harmful when they help, or helpful when they do nothing.

Hypothesis. For operations where a module-keyed lesson exists, injecting it raises the first-VALIDATE pass rate compared with withholding it.

Design (a proposal).

  • Unit: one operation that has an eligible lesson. Randomise per operation, for example on a hash of the op id, into "inject" or "withhold". Only eligible operations count, which removes the selection bias.
  • Primary metric: the verdict of the first VALIDATE, taken before micro-fix, VALIDATE_RETRY and L2 run (§93.8). Otherwise the repair ladder dilutes the effect.
  • Secondary metric: landed commits per operation, counted by the O+V trailer and confirmed in git, not the session summary, which §93.10 shows undercounts.
  • Controls: pin the served model and adapter for the whole run, because a change of adapter (§93.11) would confound the comparison. Stratify by goal kind, since test files dominate the landings (84 of 91, §93.10). Report results per module family.

Sample size, roughly. Suppose the baseline pass rate is around 40%, so the variance is about 0.24, and the target is a 10-point difference. The standard error of the difference is the square root of 2 times 0.24 divided by n per arm. Requiring two standard errors to fit inside 0.10 gives n of at least 192 per arm, so roughly 400 eligible operations in total. At soak cadence that is several soaks, which fits the supply-wall finding in §93.10: the experiment is limited by work supply, not by compute.

Falsification. If the inject arm's first-VALIDATE pass rate is not higher than the withhold arm's, beyond that noise band, the causal claim fails for LessonMemory. If it is lower, lessons are harmful, perhaps by using up prompt space (compare the bounded-context concern in §24). The test follows the same rule the operator applies to the parametric side in §93.11: no claim of learning without a held-out comparison.
