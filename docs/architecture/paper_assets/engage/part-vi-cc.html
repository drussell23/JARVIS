Quick check · §39

1. What does the CandidateProvider protocol buy the orchestrator, and what does adding a fourth provider require?

The orchestrator calls generate, plan and health_check without knowing whether it is talking to DoubleWord, Claude, J-Prime or a test mock. A new provider only has to implement the protocol and register with the GovernedLoopService; pipeline code does not change (§39.4).

2. Why does the Claude provider carry a 60-second hard fallback cap (_FALLBACK_MAX_TIMEOUT_S)?

Claude is often the fallback in a cascade. Without a cap, an unreachable provider could consume the whole pipeline budget while the operation waits on it; the cap bounds that loss (§39.2).

3. In April 2026, how did J-Prime's pricing and role differ from the two paid providers?

J-Prime cost only the VM it ran on, with no per-token billing. It was an opt-in, Tier 2 provider for bounded-volume structured-JSON work, ran at a fixed temperature of 0.2 with schema enforcement, and was disabled when unhealthy (§39.3).

Quick check · §40

1. What would go wrong if a failing primary were simply held down after an outage instead of being retried once its recovery ETA passes?

Every later operation would go to the fallback, which is usually the more expensive provider. Retrying the primary as soon as the backoff window elapses is what lets the cost optimization survive transient failures (§40, §40.4).

2. What is the difference between FALLBACK_ACTIVE and QUEUE_ONLY?

In FALLBACK_ACTIVE the primary is in backoff and work goes to the fallback directly. QUEUE_ONLY is entered when both primary and fallback fail with CONNECTION_ERROR; operations wait in the queue and should_attempt_primary() returns false until a probe succeeds (§40.3, §40.4).

3. Why does the probe interval shrink from 60 s to 5 s as the recovery ETA approaches?

Far from the ETA, recovery is unlikely and frequent probes would waste effort. Near and past the ETA, a fast probe detects recovery sooner. The paper calls this a cost optimization of the probe itself (§40.5).

Quick check · §41

1. How does a Tier 1 batch call return to its caller without anyone polling?

The Body registers an asyncio.Future under the batch_id in the BatchFutureRegistry. When DoubleWord finishes, it POSTs to /webhook/doubleword; the EventChannelServer verifies the signature, finds the future and resolves it, so the waiting coroutine simply resumes (§41.2).

2. Tier 2 still polls. In what way does it differ from the fixed 5 s polling it replaced?

It starts at 2 s, grows by 1.5x per iteration up to a 30 s cap, adds plus or minus 25% jitter, and jumps to a 15 s base on connection errors. It is used only when webhooks cannot be configured (§41.3).

Quick check · §42

1. A runtime_health signal arrives at normal (not CRITICAL) urgency with ordinary complexity. Which route does it get, and why?

STANDARD. The source is in _IMMEDIATE_SOURCES, but the IMMEDIATE branch also requires urgency of at least CRITICAL, so the signal falls through to the default cascade (§42.3).

2. The complexity check runs first in classify. What does that ordering decide?

Any heavy_code or complex operation goes to COMPLEX whatever its source or urgency, so even a background or speculative source gets the Claude-plans, DW-executes cascade when the work is heavy (§42.3).

3. What does the minimum fallback reserve in the deadline split protect against?

A slow primary consuming the entire generation deadline. Guaranteeing at least 20 s to the fallback (and at least 25 s to Tier 1) means the fallback always has runway to recover the operation (§42.6).

Quick check · §43

1. When DoubleWord was sealed, why did BACKGROUND and SPECULATIVE use skip_and_queue instead of cascade_to_claude?

Those routes are DW-only by design (BACKGROUND has no Claude fallback, SPECULATIVE is fire-and-forget batch, §42.1). Sending low-priority work to Claude would break their cost contract, so it is skipped and queued instead of escalated (§43.1).

2. What evidence led to the seal, and what would justify lifting it on a given route?

Two isolation runs on 2026-04-14 showed the same SSE stream stalled (no data for 30s) signature. Lifting requires a sustained-concurrent-load validation with the DW gateway team, then a per-route flip to dw_allowed: true with a shadow-mode comparison against Claude for a battle-test cycle (§43.2, §43.3).
