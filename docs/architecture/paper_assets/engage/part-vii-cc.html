Quick check · §44

1. Where does the reasoning: line in the CLI come from, and when is it captured?

It comes from candidate_rationales at GENERATE, stored per operation with set_op_reasoning(op_id, reasoning), and rendered under each Update(path) block in SerpentFlow. It is the model's own explanation, captured at the moment of decision (§44).

2. What does the operator gain from the rationale that the diff alone does not give?

The diff shows what changed; the rationale shows why. That makes the autonomy legible: the operator can follow the model's thinking, which the paper ties to Principle 7, Absolute Observability (§44).

Quick check · §45

1. Why does a BLOCKED operation stop at CLASSIFY instead of being generated and then rejected later?

The refusal is structural, not advisory: the orchestrator never attempts GENERATE, writes a BLOCKED ledger entry and emits a BLOCKED POSTMORTEM. No candidate for a forbidden target (supervisor, credentials, governance engine) is ever produced (§45.2).

2. What does the operator have to do for a yellow NOTIFY_APPLY operation to land?

Nothing. The change auto-applies after the 5-second window unless the operator types cancel op-id. The human is informed and can intervene, but is not required to (§45.1).

3. Which rule numbers put an operation in the yellow tier?

Rule 10 (new files), Rule 11 (multi-file changes) and Rule 12 (core orchestration paths) (§45 table).

Quick check · §46

1. Without the file-overlap check, what would happen when two sensors target the same file at once?

Two uncoordinated operations would run, and one would trample the other's changes, so the organism would produce a merge conflict with itself (§46).

2. When operation A finishes, how does a signal queued behind it get going again?

release_op(op_id_A) removes A's files from _active_file_ops and re-ingests the queued signal automatically, so it is planned against the new state that includes A's patch (§46.1, §46.2).

Quick check · §47

1. A model calls read_file on the same file four times with the same arguments. How much diversity credit do the last three calls earn?

None. Duplicate calls (same tool and same arguments_hash) get zero credit, because progress is measured as new files or queries, not repeated fetches (§47.3).

2. What happens when a candidate fails the exploration gate?

The Iron Gate, at the post-GENERATE, pre-VALIDATE checkpoint, returns a GENERATE_RETRY with targeted feedback naming the required exploration. The paper reports the model typically explores properly on the retry (§47.2).

3. Why do trivial operations bypass the ledger while architectural ones must cover call_graph and history?

The floors scale with risk. A trivial change does not justify the exploration cost, but rewriting core code requires knowing who calls it and why it was written that way (§47.3).

Quick check · §48

1. Why does VERIFY run only the tests that exercise the changed files instead of the full suite?

The full suite would be slow. A scope targeted to the diff catches regressions in the changed code within the 60 s JARVIS_VERIFY_TIMEOUT_S budget (§48.1).

2. VERIFY fails and L2 repair also fails. What happens to the applied change?

The failure is combined with the benchmark result and the change is rolled back (§48.1).

Quick check · §49

1. How does a lesson from one operation reach the next one?

The orchestrator's _session_lessons buffer is copied into OperationContext.session_lessons before each GENERATE and rendered as a "Session Lessons" section in the generation prompt (§49.1, §49.2).

2. How large can the buffer get, and how long does it live?

At most 20 lessons (JARVIS_SESSION_LESSONS_MAX). It is ephemeral and lasts for the session, and the convergence check can clear it early (§49.1, §49.4).

Quick check · §50

1. In the _compute_priority formula, is a larger number served sooner or later? Which terms push a signal earlier?

Later: a lower number means higher priority. Urgency boost, the confidence bonus and dependency credit are subtracted, so they move a signal earlier; the file-count cost penalty is added, so sprawling signals move later (§50.1).

2. Why is the score computed on retry enqueue and WAL replay, not only on first enqueue?

So that a signal's place in the queue does not depend on how it entered it. A deferred or recovered signal is ordered by the same rule as a fresh one (§50.3).

Quick check · §51

1. Which three capabilities together give "concurrent operations do not conflict, each reads before writing, and each change is verified in situ"?

The dependency DAG (§46), exploration-first enforcement (§47) and the post-apply verification loop (§48) (§51).

2. What three properties does the paper claim every one of the seven capabilities has?

Each is battle-test validated (Part XII), observable (Principle 7) and reversible through environment variables (§51).
