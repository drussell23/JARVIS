Connections

Connection 1 (Thermodynamics as an examined analogy, with information theory). §92.17 treats the shell Sn as a "deterministic-entropy reservoir" and conjectures that (a) its deterministic entropy never decreases, (b) the entropy of the core's output conditioned on Sn never increases, and (c) net agentic entropy stays bounded. Test the analogy on a toy model. All of the following are assumptions of the toy, not figures from the paper: the candidate patches for one task form a space of 220 equally likely patches; each shell constraint independently rejects 5% of them; the shell is a pure conjunction, so a patch is accepted only if it passes every constraint. (1) Write the Boltzmann-style entropy Hk = log2|Ak| of the accepted set after k constraints. (2) Evaluate it for k = 22 (the Iron Gate's 12 rules plus the Semantic Guardian's 10 detectors) and for k = 543 (adding the 521 AST pins of §92.11, treated in the toy as filters on candidates). (3) Say where the thermodynamic analogy holds, where it breaks, and what the toy suggests the real formal target is.

Hint

Each constraint multiplies the accepted fraction by 0.95, so it adds log20.95 ≈ −0.074 bits to H. Then ask whether property (c) can fail at all in a finite space.

Worked solution

(1) |Ak| = 220 × 0.95k, so Hk = 20 + k log20.95 = 20 − 0.0740k bits. It falls by a fixed amount with every constraint added.

(2) At k = 22: 0.9522 ≈ 0.324, so about a third of the space survives and H ≈ 20 − 1.63 = 18.37 bits. At k = 543: 0.95543 = e543 × ln 0.95 = e−27.9 ≈ 8 × 10−13. The expected accepted set is 220 × 8 × 10−13 ≈ 10−6 patches, so in practice it is empty. The formula gives 20 − 40.2 ≈ −20 bits, which only means the set is gone. Under independent constraints the shell crushes the core.

Where the analogy holds. Entropy as the logarithm of the number of accessible states (S = kB ln W) is real statistical mechanics. "Constraints remove accessible states" is exactly what the shell does. Property (b) holds here, and in any purely conjunctive shell: if An+1 ⊆ An, then log|An+1| ≤ log|An|.

Where it breaks. First, (a) and (b) are not a reservoir and a source exchanging entropy. They are one fact counted twice with opposite signs: adding constraints is what shrinks the accessible set. Calling the constraint count "deterministic entropy" uses the word for a quantity that grows as the physical entropy falls. Second, the second law is about an isolated system evolving on its own. Sn grows only because M deliberately adds validated patches. There is no temperature, no conserved energy and no spontaneous process, so no thermodynamic theorem transfers. Third, (c) is trivially true in a finite space, since H can never exceed log2|A0| = 20 bits, so as stated it has no content.

The real target. The toy shows that the dangerous direction is the opposite of the one (c) bounds. The risk is that An collapses to nothing, the case §92.17 itself names when it says the immune system must scale "so it never crushes the core". A formal statement worth proving is therefore a two-sided one. Entropy must stay below a ceiling (containment), and the probability mass the core places inside An must stay above a floor (capability). That floor can hold only if constraints are strongly correlated with what good patches already do, or if the core's distribution moves toward An as fast as the shell tightens. Independence, as in this toy, makes it impossible.

Connection 2 (Markov chains). §92.12 and §92.17 cite Wang's formulation of RSI as a Markov chain on capability states with a stationary transition kernel, and its conditions include a positive trend and a non-decreasing trajectory. Take a toy chain, whose numbers are assumptions and not from the paper. The states are 0 (regressed), 1 (baseline) and 2 (improved), and the rows of the kernel P are: from 0, (0.5, 0.5, 0); from 1, (0.1, 0.6, 0.3); from 2, (0, 0.4, 0.6). (1) Find the stationary distribution π and the long-run expected capability. (2) Starting in state 1, compute E[X1], E[X2] and E[X3], and find the rate at which the trend dies away. (3) Make state 2 absorbing and find the expected number of steps to reach it from state 1. (4) Explain what this says about why §92.17 needs a "non-stationary-but-monotone" extension.

Hint

The chain only moves to neighbouring states, so detailed balance (πiPi,i+1 = πi+1Pi+1,i) gives π directly. For the decay rate, the trace of P and its determinant give the two non-unit eigenvalues.

Worked solution

(1) Detailed balance gives π0 × 0.5 = π1 × 0.1, so π0 = 0.2π1. It also gives π1 × 0.3 = π2 × 0.4, so π2 = 0.75π1. Normalising: 1.95π1 = 1, so π ≈ (0.103, 0.513, 0.385). The long-run expected capability is 0.513 + 2 × 0.385 ≈ 1.282.

(2) From state 1, the distribution after one step is (0.1, 0.6, 0.3), so E[X1] = 1.2. After two steps it is (0.11, 0.53, 0.36), so E[X2] = 1.25. After three it is (0.108, 0.517, 0.375), so E[X3] ≈ 1.267. The increments are 0.2, 0.05 and 0.017, and they shrink toward zero. The trace of P is 1.7 and its determinant is 0.09, so the non-unit eigenvalues solve x2 − 0.7x + 0.09 = 0. That gives x ≈ 0.530 and 0.170. The gap to equilibrium shrinks by about 0.53 per step, so after 10 steps less than 0.2% of it is left. The "trend" is only a transient relaxation to equilibrium.

(3) Make state 2 absorbing and let ti be the expected steps to absorption. Then t0 = 1 + 0.5t0 + 0.5t1, which gives t0 = 2 + t1. And t1 = 1 + 0.1t0 + 0.6t1, which gives 0.3t1 = 1.2, so t1 = 4 and t0 = 6. Now capability reaches 2 with probability 1 and stays there.

(4) A stationary, ergodic chain cannot show sustained improvement. Its expected capability converges to a constant, and its trajectory keeps fluctuating, including downward. Sustained improvement needs either an absorbing ratchet or a kernel that changes over time. The absorbing state needs a fixed top level and assumes no regression. A changing kernel is exactly what §92.17 says O+V has, because M depends on the whole history through the ExplorationLedger and the SemanticIndex.

The "monotone" half of the proposed extension plays the role of the ratchet. It is the requirement that Sn+1 strictly extends Sn. The toy shows why that requirement carries the whole argument: with it, the chain behaves like (3), and without it, like (2). It is also the assumption to check against the record. Reverted landings and DARK features (§93.10, §93.12) are regressions that a strict ratchet would not allow.
