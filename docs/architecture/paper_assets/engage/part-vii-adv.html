Advanced conceptual questions

A1. Exploration-first has now been enforced at three levels: a prompt instruction (§47.1), a post-GENERATE gate with GENERATE_RETRY (§47.2, §47.3), and in October a constrained-decoding grammar that allows only tool calls until the minimum exploration is met (§93.6). What does each level fail to guarantee, and why did the 30B need the third?

The prompt (§47.1) guarantees nothing. It is a request, and §47.2 says plainly that "a model can ignore instructions." The gate (§47.2, §47.3) guarantees that no candidate gets past GENERATE without enough exploration. It does this after the fact, though: the model has to produce a non-compliant answer, get rejected, and retry. That works for a model that improves when given feedback. §47.5 shows Claude-era Session G doing exactly that, going from score=3.00 to score=25.50 across five categories.

The 30B did not improve on retry. §93.6 records 106 in-loop rejections and zero candidates in bt-2026-08-31-155308. A gate that only rejects cannot help a model that never produces a compliant answer, so every retry burned budget and produced the same failure. The grammar moves enforcement from rejecting the output to restricting what the model can generate at all: an answer without exploration becomes impossible to emit.

The grammar has its own gap. It guarantees that tool calls happen, not that they are relevant or diverse. The A/B in §93.6 shows how a model behaves under it. With {tool, noop} allowed, the 30B chose noop 3 out of 3 times: it took the cheapest legal exit. Only with {tool} alone did it explore 3 out of 3. So the diversity scoring and anti-gaming rules of §47.3 and §47.4 still do work that the grammar cannot. The three levels are complementary, not redundant.

A2. §49 describes session lessons, and §49.4 adds a convergence metric. §93.8 reports LessonMemory: 1,115 module-keyed lessons, with "424 injections followed by a VALIDATE pass." Does either figure show that lessons help? Argue a position and name the evidence that would settle it.

Neither figure shows that lessons help. "424 injections were followed by a VALIDATE pass" is a count of co-occurrences. It has no denominator of passes without injection and no comparison arm. If the base VALIDATE pass rate on similar operations were the same, 424 would be exactly what you would expect with lessons having no effect.

The §49.4 metric does compare something: success rates before and after the first lesson. But the two windows differ in more than the lessons. They cover different operations, at different points in a session, and possibly with different queue mixes (the §50 priority sends critical, focused work first, so later work may be harder). A drop after the first lesson can therefore reflect harder work, not bad lessons, and the buffer gets cleared anyway.

A defensible position is that lessons are plausible but unproven. The design is still sound because it is cheap and fails safe: the infra guard (§49.3) and the clear-on-regression rule (§49.4) cap the damage. The evidence that would settle it is a randomized withhold: for each eligible operation, inject or withhold lessons by coin flip, and compare VALIDATE pass rates (and landings) between the arms over operations drawn from the same queue. This is the same standard §93.11 applies to the trained adapter, where a held-out gain is required before anything counts as learning.

A3. §45.1 calls NOTIFY_APPLY a "trust-building mechanism": a human watches a 5 s window and refines the rules when something slips through. In October, Autonomous Sentinel Mode auto-approves up to APPROVAL_REQUIRED (§93.7). What happens to that trust-building loop, and what replaced it?

The §45.1 loop has three parts: a human sees the change, has a window to cancel, and refines the rules after a bad Yellow operation slips through. Each part assumes someone is watching. Under Sentinel Mode the organism finds its own work, signs it, and auto-approves up to the tier ceiling. By default that ceiling is APPROVAL_REQUIRED (§93.7), which means even Orange operations proceed without a human. The 5 s window still runs, but in an unattended soak nobody types cancel, so the loop no longer has its observer.

The design replaced live human vigilance with checks that run after the fact and can be undone. Work lands in a sovereignty worktree, accumulates on a session branch, and must pass a promotion gate that checks provenance, coverage, and that no public symbol vanished. Survivors are fast-forwarded onto local main only. A zero-push air-gap keeps publishing an operator act (§93.7). BLOCKED operations, self-modification of the governance layer, and anything touching the cage still escalate to a human.

The "refine the rules" step survived, but it now happens offline. §93.14 item 2 lists wrong landings: one hollow, one with no intended change, one that dropped content, and a documentation edit that flattened text. It says the promotion gate and the Test Reality Gate "exist because of these." That is the §45.1 loop with a longer delay: a human still finds the slip and hardens a rule, but after the commit is already on local main, not during a 5 s window. The trade-off is unattended throughput in exchange for later detection, and the air-gap is what keeps that later detection safe.

Challenge problems

Challenge 1 (cross-Part trace). October system (§93.6): J-Prime is the only lane. A doc_staleness operation is active on parser.py. A test_failure signal at CRITICAL urgency then arrives for parser.py only, with confidence 0.95 and complexity simple. Trace it to the end: intake (§52.8, §50, §46), routing (§42.3, §42.5), GENERATE (§93.6, §47.3), APPLY and VERIFY (§48, §52.3), and the lesson it leaves (§49). Along the way, the model's first attempt makes two identical read_file calls on parser.py. Later, the scoped post-apply tests fail once. Name every gate that acts and what it does.

Hint

Duplicates earn zero credit (§47.3). Ask what simple requires. Also ask which enqueue path the test-failure signal takes when the doc operation releases its lock (§50.3).

Worked solution

Intake. Coalescing (§52.8) would merge same-file signals within 30 s, but HIGH-urgency signals bypass it. Treating CRITICAL as at least HIGH, the signal is not held. Priority (§50.1): base 2 for test_failure (as in §50.2), minus 3 for critical, plus 0 for one file, minus 1 for confidence at or above 0.9, minus 0 dependency credit, giving −2. The DAG (§46) finds parser.py active under the doc operation, so the signal waits in _queued_behind. When the doc operation completes, release_op re-ingests it, and the retry enqueue path recomputes the same priority (§50.3), so it goes to the front.

Routing. Complexity simple is not in _COMPLEX_COMPLEXITIES, and the source is not speculative or background. It is an immediate source at CRITICAL, so §42.3 returns IMMEDIATE, with a 60 s plus 5 s generation budget (§42.5). In October the free-lane policy serves that route from the local engine (§93.6).

GENERATE. Tool masking allows only tool calls until the minimum exploration is met (§93.6). The model calls read_file(parser.py) twice. The second call is a duplicate and earns zero credit (§47.3). The ledger then sees one COMPREHENSION call. The simple floor is a score of 4.0 and two categories, so it fails. The Iron Gate raises GENERATE_RETRY with targeted feedback (§47.2). On retry the model adds, for example, get_callers (CALL_GRAPH), reaches two categories and enough score, and produces a candidate. All of this has to fit inside the 65 s IMMEDIATE budget, so the duplicate cost real time.

Risk. One file and not a core path, so the operation is SAFE_AUTO and applies silently (§45). Because it was generated after the doc operation released the file, the GENERATE-time hashes match at APPLY (§52.7), and the stale guard stays quiet.

VERIFY. The scoped tests fail once (§48.1). VERIFY builds a synthetic ValidationResult and calls L2. Under Refinement 10 the L2 fix is written to disk with change_engine.execute() before VERIFY runs again (§52.3). If L2 converges, the operation completes. If not, the result is combined with the benchmark result and rolled back.

Lesson. If it completes, an [OK] lesson enters the buffer. If it rolls back on a test failure, a [FAIL:test] lesson is tagged "code" and injected into later prompts (§49.1, §49.2). If it died on a timeout, the lesson is tagged "infra" and excluded (§49.3).

Check. The gates that acted were coalescing-bypass, DAG queueing, the priority recompute, the router, tool masking, the exploration ledger and its retry, the risk tier, the stale-hash guard, VERIFY with L2, and lesson tagging. Each is cited to the section that defines it. Nothing here required a mechanism the paper does not describe.

Challenge 2 (adversarial). Act as a lazy model under the ExplorationLedger. Your target is a moderate operation on billing.py. The floor is a score of at least 8.0 across at least 3 categories (§47.3). Construct a tool-call sequence that passes the gate while learning nothing about billing.py. Then determine which layer in Parts VII and VIII, or in §93, stops the resulting patch, and whether any of them stops it because exploration was shallow.

Hint

Read §47.3 and §47.4 for what is checked: distinct tool and argument pairs, categories, and success. Is the target file named anywhere in the gate condition, as opposed to in the prompt (§47.1)?

Worked solution

The attack. The ledger awards credit to distinct, successful calls and counts categories (§47.3, §47.4). It checks three conditions: score, category count, and required categories (none for moderate). None of them mentions the target file. A sequence like this meets the shape:

  1. read_file(README.md) for COMPREHENSION.
  2. list_dir(docs/) and glob_files("*.cfg") for DISCOVERY.
  3. git_log on an unrelated path for HISTORY.
  4. More distinct, successful reads of unrelated files until the score clears 8.0.

The paper does not publish per-call weights, so the number of extra calls is unknown. The anti-gaming rules do not block any of this. Duplicates earn zero credit, but every call here is distinct. Failed calls do not cover categories, but every call here succeeds. Three categories are covered and the score rises with each new call.

Who stops it? The prompt (§47.1) asks for the target file to be read, but §47.2 counts any read_file. Tool masking (§93.6) forces tool calls, not relevant ones. The stale-hash guard (§52.7) checks concurrency, not understanding. So nothing stops the shallow exploration itself. What stops a bad patch are the outcome gates. VALIDATE runs tests against the candidate. VERIFY reruns scoped tests on disk (§48). In October, the Test Reality Gate rejects tests that never import their subject or assert nothing, and the promotion gate refuses a landing where a public symbol vanished (§93.7, §93.8). The AST-Signature Anchor also injects the real module API whether or not the model explored (§93.8), which makes a blind patch less likely to invent functions.

Honest verdict. No layer catches shallow exploration as such. A patch that is shallow but correct passes everything. That is arguably acceptable, because the outcome gates are authoritative and exploration is a means, not an end. §93.6 also shows this pressure is real: given {tool, noop}, the 30B chose noop every time, so models do take the cheapest legal path.

A proposal (not in the paper). Require that at least one COMPREHENSION credit names a path in the operation's target files, and for architectural, that the CALL_GRAPH credit names a target symbol. Check the fix by replaying the attack sequence above: it should now fail on the new required condition while an honest sequence that starts by reading billing.py still passes.
