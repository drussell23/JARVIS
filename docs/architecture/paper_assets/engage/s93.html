Pause and predict

Reactor has trained a LoRA adapter on O+V's own recorded attempts, and J-Prime serves it. Predict whether that adapter made the model measurably better, and what evidence would actually count as proof.

Reveal the answer

No improvement has been shown. The adapter beat the incumbent 4/6 to 3/6 on its gate. The project's own notes reject that as evidence of learning: three contributing answers, a gradient norm of about 1e-4, and one greedy flip. The evidence that counts is a held-out benchmark: 30 tasks drawn from human commits with hidden tests. There the adapter was accepted on 1/30 or 3/30, solved 0, and showed a mean gain of 0.03. Until a held-out gain appears, no training-derived change is pushed. See §93.11.

#### Check your understanding: §93 — Developments (May 14 – October 13, 2026)

Conceptual questions

1. Why does §93 count autonomous landings by a commit trailer on main, rather than by what each soak session reports in its summary?

The summaries were measured to be wrong. PRD §34 records session summaries undercounting landings (2 against 3, and 1 against 10), so "the ledger and git, not the summary, are the source of truth" (§93.10). A trailer such as Co-Authored-By: Ouroboros+Venom lives in the repository itself, so anyone can recount it with git log. A session log can be lost, misread or simply wrong. The same thinking runs through the rest of the design. GoalReconciliationLedger binds goals to commits using git ancestry, not logs (§93.7), and landing provenance attaches git-proven landed/surviving labels to training rows (§93.11). The rule also forces honesty in the other direction. The four July Run-22/23 commits carry the trailer too, so they are listed separately, not quietly folded in or dropped.

2. Every landing has already passed VALIDATE and its module's tests before it reaches the promotion gate. Why does the gate's structural "no public symbol vanished" check still matter?

The local model's most common bad output is a whole-file re-emission: it rewrites the file instead of producing a diff and reproduces most of it. Tests rarely assert that a given export or __all__ entry still exists, so a lost symbol can pass every test. §93.7 says the structural check is the one that caught such a whole-file re-emission. §93.10 shows the damage that reaches main without such checks: one landing where only a punctuation rewrite landed (reverted), and one that dropped content later restored by hand. The root fix is diff output, and that is still the leading failure mode, with 219 whole-file answers against 6 diffs in the 10-09 soak (§93.8, §93.14). Until that is fixed, a cheap mechanical check against the lane's known regression class is what keeps passing tests from approving a damaged file.

3. What does the "supply wall" imply about how to raise landed-commit yield, and why does the plan in §93.15 delay arming the Epistemic Planner?

The supply wall means that after about four hours on a diet of uncovered modules, yield is bounded by how much landable work exists, not by the model (§93.10). In the 6.03 h soak, nothing landed from 22:24 to 00:29. A bigger model or more decode slots would not fix that, because the queue of suitable work is the bottleneck. §93.15 therefore lists widening supply as step 3: evidence-ranked red tests and sanctioned model proposals. Arming the Epistemic Planner, a new source of model-invented work, comes only after diffs land, a held-out gain is measured and supply is widened. Otherwise the backlog would fill faster than it drains. Adding work the organism cannot yet land well would only grow the queue of failures.

Problem 2 (analysis). The bt-2026-10-05-012717 soak ran 6.03 h with 6 substantive landings, at 1.34 substantive landings per hour over its first 2.98 h. Estimate how many substantive landings came in the first 2.98 h and what rate the remaining time achieved. Then explain what this pattern does and does not tell you about the 30B model.

Hint

Multiply the rate by the time, subtract from 6, and divide by the remaining hours. Then reread the "supply wall" lesson.

Worked solution

First window: 1.34 per hour × 2.98 h ≈ 4.0, so about 4 substantive landings. Remainder: 6 − 4 = 2 landings over 6.03 − 2.98 = 3.05 h, about 0.66 per hour. The second half ran at roughly half the rate, and the session table notes that nothing landed between 22:24 and 00:29.

The model did not change during the soak, so the decline is not evidence that the model got worse. PRD §34 reads it as the supply wall: after about four hours on uncovered modules, yield is bounded by the supply of landable work (§93.10).

It also does not show that the model is strong in general. 84 of 91 landings are test files, and production landings at cadence are listed as not demonstrated (§93.14). The fix the pattern points to is wider supply, step 3 in §93.15, not more slots. The slot A/B was inconclusive anyway.

Problem 3 (code reading). Open backend/core/ouroboros/governance/accumulation_promotion_gate.py and read _public_surface and _check_structure. Work out what counts as the "public surface", which direction of change is refused, how a file that no longer parses is treated, and whether a commit that drops an export can pass if a later commit on the branch restores it.

Hint

The module docstring calls this a "SHRINK check", and _check_structure's docstring says what it is "judged across".

Worked solution

_public_surface parses the source and collects module-level function, async-function and class names, plus assignment targets, that do not start with an underscore. It also collects the declared __all__ by literal_eval. It returns None if the source does not parse.

_check_structure compares the surface before and after for each touched .py file. It is a shrink check. New public symbols are "ordinary work". Lost names, lost __all__ entries, or a removed __all__ produce a failing Finding. Added or deleted files are skipped, because they are not a shrink.

A file that does not parse after the commit fails immediately with "does not parse after the commit". A file that did not parse before is skipped.

The check is judged across the range, from base, not commit by commit. A drop followed by a restore on the same branch is "together, no loss". The module docstring adds that the gate fails closed and that a refusal leaves the accumulation branch in place as the quarantined, reviewable artifact. This matches §93.7, where this check caught a whole-file re-emission that tests could not see.
