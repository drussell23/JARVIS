Quick check · §61

1. What two conditions end a battle-test session, and what values does the example invocation use for them?

The session ends when the cost cap is hit, or when the idle timeout passes with no operations. The example uses --cost-cap 0.50, which is 0.50 US dollars, and --idle-timeout 600, which is 600 seconds. See §61.

2. The letter labels (A, B, C...) are "post-hoc". What does a reader need in order to check a claim about, say, Session O?

The bt-YYYY-MM-DD-HHMMSS session ID, which this Part maps to each letter. The letters are not stamped into the session at run time. The ID names the .ouroboros/sessions/bt-<timestamp>/ directory that holds debug.log. See §61.1 and §61.2.

Quick check · §62

1. Session A made four read_file calls and scored 3.00 in one category. Why would the legacy gate have passed it?

The legacy gate checked tool_calls >= 2. It counted calls, and four is at least two. The ledger weights the diversity of exploration, so four reads of one kind score low. See §62 and §62.1.

2. Session C landed nothing. Why does the paper still list it?

It proved the instrumentation from commit 614009ec05, the Track 1 and Track 2 diagnostics. The paper says that without it the wrong diagnosis would have persisted. Measurement came before further fixes. See §62.3.

3. Session G passed at 25.50, and Session O later passed at 11.00. How can such different scores both pass?

The ledger compares the score with a floor set by the complexity tier, not with a fixed target. Commit 4f60a584f9 set the complex-tier floor at 10.0. Any score above the floor passes. See §62.6, §62.5 and §63.2.

Quick check · §63

1. Sessions I, L and M were resolved with environment overrides, not commits. What does that say about those failures?

They were limits that real load exceeded, not wrong code: a too_many_files risk escalation, a 900s pool ceiling and a 180s fallback cap. Raising the thresholds was enough. That is also why the arc made such thresholds tunable from the environment (commit 5d169266d6). See §63.1.

2. Session O's first attempt scored 0.00 on the ExplorationLedger. What turned it into a pass?

The retry injected sharpened feedback, and the second attempt scored 11.00 across four categories with would_pass=True. See §63.2.

3. Session O recorded two applied DECISIONs for one operation. Why two?

The first applied the candidate. VERIFY then found test critiques, L2 Repair converged on iteration 1 of 5, and the repaired result was applied in a second DECISION. POSTMORTEM then reported root_cause=none. See §63.2.

Quick check · §64

1. What was the original bug that Session Q isolated?

The parser rejected multi-file candidates with schema_invalid:candidate_0_missing_file_path. It expected a single-file shape. Session R then verified the parser fix with a 4-file candidate. See §64.1 and §64.2.

2. Session R died at the exploration gate with 0 fresh tool calls. Why did the paper not count that as a multi-file failure?

The 4-file candidate had already passed the parser, so the multi-file path worked. The rejection came from the op running right after another and making no fresh exploration calls: a consecutive-op issue. See §64.2.

3. Why did Session S run with JARVIS_EXPLORATION_GATE=false?

To isolate Gate 5, multi-file coverage. Session R had just shown that the exploration gate could stop an op before coverage was ever tested. Turning it off let the session test one mechanism at a time. See §64.2 and §64.3.

Quick check · §65

1. In Session U, the same candidate was 'test' on iteration 0 and 'infra' on iteration 1. Why was that fatal, not just noisy?

An 'infra' result routes through the non-retryable _early_return_ctx branch. A sandbox flake on the re-validation therefore ended the op, even though the real problem was a fixable LSP defect. See §65.2.

2. The fix gives L2 a fresh deadline. Why does it also reconcile ctx.pipeline_deadline upward?

So that phases after L2 see a consistent clock for the op. Without that, L2 would hold 600s while the op's own deadline still read 0.0s, and later phases would see two clocks that disagree. See §65.4.

3. How did the paper confirm Session W's four files landed, and what independent check of their quality followed?

Disk state was verified by mtime, and the files matched. A separate pytest run then passed 20 of 20 tests against the real TestFailureSensor, not a mock, and covered the four behaviours the backlog task asked for. See §65.4.

Quick check · §66

1. The T→U and V→W episodes are counted as proof of what?

Of §8 Absolute Observability working as a diagnostic loop: hypothesize, instrument, falsify or confirm, fix, and verify in one session. FSM instrumentation disproved the silent-exit idea in one session, and one INFO line named the L2 budget bug. See §66.1.

2. §66.1 claims only the "enforcement half" of §6 Threshold-Triggered Neuroplasticity. What half is missing?

Durability. §6 asks for three consecutive successes before a capability graduates, and Session W is one. See §66.1 and §66.2.

3. The stale phase=CLASSIFY in cost_governor.finish only affects a log. Why is it still on the list of load-bearing items?

The paper calls it display-only, but also "a real ctx-reference inconsistency": the governor reads an outdated phase from the context. Such an inconsistency is a deferred defect, not a fix. See §66.2.

Quick check · §67

1. What makes the breakthrough log a "falsifiability trail"?

It records each hypothesis, observed failure, added instrumentation, fix commit, cost and duration, and quotes the debug logs. A reader who disputes a claim can go to the session and read the raw evidence. See §67.

2. How does this differ from the per-operation observability of earlier Parts?

It covers the system's whole developmental history, not single operations. The paper calls this Principle 7 at the system level, a "fossil record". See §67.
